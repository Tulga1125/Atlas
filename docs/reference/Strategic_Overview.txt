The Big Picture —
Atlas & Atom
What Atlas is trying to become, how the eight businesses relate to each other, and

What Atlas Is
Atlas is not a media company in the conventional sense. It is a platform — a set
of interlocking businesses that, together, control how Mongolian culture is
documented, distributed, and monetised.

Most media companies in Mongolia own content. Atlas's ambition is different: to own the
infrastructure beneath the content — the charts that rank it, the identifiers that track it, the data
that proves what it's worth, and the platforms that publish it. This is a fundamentally more
defensible position, because infrastructure compounds over time in ways that content alone
cannot.



    The analogy is this: Apple doesn't just sell music. Apple built the App Store,
    the payment rails, and the discovery algorithm. The content flows through
    infrastructure Apple controls. Atlas is building that same infrastructure layer
    for Mongolian culture.

The Moat —
Why ISRCs Change Everything
A plain-language explanation of what an ISRC is, why controlling the Mongolian
registry is such a significant long-term advantage, and where the path leads from
there.




What an ISRC Actually Is
An ISRC — International Standard Recording Code — is the permanent identity
number assigned to every song recording in the world. It looks like this: MN-
ATL-25-00001.

Think of it like a passport number for a song. Every time a song gets played on Spotify, counted
on a chart, matched on radio, or licensed for a TV commercial anywhere in the world — the
system uses the ISRC to know which song it is, who owns it, and who gets paid for it. Without an
ISRC, a song is essentially invisible to the international music industry infrastructure.


    BREAKING IT DOWN

    MN — Country code. Mongolia. Controlled by IFPI (the global music industry body) and only
    assigned to the official national agency.


    ATL — Registrant code. This is the three-letter code assigned to a label or artist by the national
    agency. If Atlas is the national agency, Atlas assigns these.


    25 — Year the code was issued (2025).


    00001 — Sequential number. Each registrant can issue up to 99,999 codes per year.



Right now, Mongolian artists get their ISRCs through international distributors like DistroKid or
TuneCore. Those distributors have their own codes — the ISRCs they issue might look like US-
UR1-25-00001, with a US country code. This means Mongolian music is not identifiable as
Mongolian in any international system. There is no coherent national catalog.

Why Controlling the Registry Is a Permanent Advantage
If Atlas becomes the IFPI-designated national ISRC agency for Mongolia, it means:

 • Every Mongolian label, distributor, and artist that wants an official MN-coded ISRC must register
    through Atlas. This is not optional — it's how the international system works.

 • Atlas becomes the keeper of the permanent, immutable historical record of every Mongolian
    recording. This database compounds in value every year — nobody can take it away or replicate it.

 • Atlas knows every new Mongolian song before it is released publicly. The ISRC registry is populated
    before distribution, which means Atlas Charts can track new releases from day one.

 • The registry database powers Atlas Charts directly. No competitor can match the data depth
    without going through Atlas.

 • IFPI designation is internationally recognised. Mongolian government bodies treat the national
    agency as an official cultural institution — this opens grant funding, policy influence, and co-
    regulation.



    The analogy here is domain name registration. Whoever controls the .mn
    domain registry controls a toll-booth on Mongolian internet infrastructure.
    Atlas controlling the ISRC registry is the music industry equivalent.



The Path from ISRC to Full Royalty Infrastructure
ISRC codes cover recordings — the specific recorded version of a song. There is a parallel system
for compositions — the underlying song, the lyrics and melody — called ISWC codes. These are
managed through Performing Rights Organisations (PROs).

Mongolia currently has no active PRO. This means every time a Mongolian song is played at a
café, on the radio, or in a shopping mall, the royalty that should flow to the songwriter simply
disappears — there is no collection mechanism. Whoever founds the Mongolian PRO collects
those royalties, forever.


   T H E LO N G G A M E

   ISRC agency → IFPI national group membership → PRO formation (or partnership). This is a 3–5
   year arc, but the ISRC registry is the first step that makes everything else possible. You cannot
   establish a PRO credibly without first having the recording identity infrastructure in place.

The Association —
Building MPIA
Why you cannot apply to IFPI as Atlas Media LLC, what you need to set up instead, and
exactly who to recruit and what to tell them.




Why You Need an Association, Not Just a Company
IFPI does not designate individual companies as national ISRC agencies. They designate national
phonographic industry associations — bodies that represent the recording industry in their
country. Every national agency you've heard of is an association: BPI (British Phonographic
Industry), RIAA (Recording Industry Association of America), ARIA (Australian Recording Industry
Association).

The distinction matters because it signals that the role is about serving the industry, not
extracting value from it. IFPI will not hand control of a country's ISRC infrastructure to one
commercial company operating alone — they need to see multiple industry voices.


    W H AT YO U ' R E B U I L D I N G

    Name: Mongolian Phonographic Industry Association (MPIA) — Монголын Дуу Авианы
    Бичлэгийн Үйлдвэрийн Холбоо


    Legal structure: ТББ (Төрийн бус байгууллага) — registered as an NGO under Mongolia's Law
    on Non-Governmental Organizations at the Ministry of Justice.


    Practical reality: Atlas Media is the founding anchor, provides all administrative support,
    secretariat, and technical infrastructure. The other founding members add the industry breadth
    IFPI requires. Atlas retains strategic control through the Chairman role.

What IFPI Wants to See
The application is straightforward once you have the right components assembled. IFPI is not
suspicious or adversarial — Mongolia is a small market with no competing claimants. They want
to see:

 • A registered legal entity (ТББ registration from Ministry of Justice, translated to English)

 • A charter with the right clauses — specifically that ISRC codes will be available to all Mongolian
    entities regardless of MPIA membership, and that the association will report annually to IFPI

 • Multiple industry members — not just one company

 • A working technical system — screenshots or a live demo of the Atom registry database. This is
    the most persuasive document in the application.

 • A Ministry of Culture letter if you can get one — not required, but it transforms the application
    from a startup to a government-backed institution



   THE KEY INSIGHT

   Mongolia currently has no national ISRC agency. There is no competing application. The country
   code MN is essentially unclaimed. IFPI will be glad to have the gap filled by a credible applicant.
   Your job is to look credible — and the founding member list, the Ministry letter, and Anand's
   working database are exactly what makes you look credible.

The Charts —
How Atlas Charts Works
Where the streaming data comes from, how the chart formula works in plain terms,
what fraud detection does, and why being an audited chart is so valuable.




Where the Data Comes From
A music chart is only as good as the data behind it. The Atlas Charts Engine
collects data from four types of sources every day — automatically, without
anyone having to do anything manually.


   S O U R C E 1 — S T R E A M I N G P L AT F O R M S

   YouTube, Spotify, Apple Music, and Deezer each report how many times every song was
   streamed in Mongolia. The Charts Engine collects this data every morning through official APIs.
   YouTube is the dominant platform in Mongolia and is publicly accessible. Spotify and Apple Music
   require formal business partnerships — those come later, once MPIA is established.




   S O U R C E 2 — R A D I O A I R P L AY ( A C R C LO U D )

   ACRCloud is a service that listens to Mongolian radio stations 24 hours a day and uses audio
   fingerprinting to identify every song that plays, on every station. The Charts Engine receives a
   daily report of every spin, on every monitored station. This airplay data is essential — Billboard
   and IFPI require it for official chart recognition. Without radio data, a chart is classified as a
   streaming chart, not a full-format chart.




   S O U R C E 3 — D O W N LOA D S & P H Y S I C A L S A L E S

   Digital downloads (from iTunes, Bandcamp) and physical sales (CDs, vinyl — small in Mongolia
   but counted) are imported weekly. These are weighted more heavily than streams per unit
   because they represent a deliberate purchase decision, not passive listening.




How a Chart Is Made, In Plain Terms
Every Monday morning at midnight, the Charts Engine runs through a seven-step process that
takes the raw data from the previous week and produces the final chart by Sunday night.

   Mon–Fri         Every day at 2am, the system automatically pulls stream counts from each platform, airplay
                   logs from ACRCloud, and any new sales data.

     Daily         Each raw track is matched to its ISRC code via the Atlas registry. This is how the system
                   knows that "Тэнгэрийн Замаар on YouTube" and "Tengeriin Zamaar on Spotify" are the

       3           Remove fraud
     Daily         Automated rules detect suspicious streaming patterns — sudden 10× spikes, streams all
                   coming from the same location, accounts created the day before all streaming the same
                   song. Suspicious streams are removed before the chart calculation runs.


       4           Calculate chart points
  Sat night        Each clean stream is converted to chart points using the weighted formula. A Spotify stream
                   is worth more than a YouTube video stream (more intentional listening). A paid download is
                   worth 150 streams. Radio plays are counted by audience reach (a station with 500,000
                   listeners counts for more than one with 50,000).


       5           Rank & review
  Sat night        All songs are ranked by total chart points. The system flags anything unusual for a final
                   human check — you, or whoever is the chart editor, reviews the top 100 before publication.
                   Any fraud decisions that need a human eye get resolved here.


       6           Publish
Sun midnight       The final chart goes live on the Atlas Charts website and API. Media partners (news sites,
                   radio stations) receive the chart automatically via webhook. The chart is cached and
                   instantly fast for anyone accessing it.


       7           Archive & audit trail
   Ongoing         Every chart is permanently archived. Every data point has a timestamp and a verifiable
                   record. An independent auditor can verify any historical chart at any time — this is the
                   requirement for IFPI recognition.



What Fraud Detection Actually Does
Chart manipulation is a real problem globally. Labels and managers sometimes buy fake streams
to inflate their artists' positions. The Atlas fraud detection system protects against this
automatically.




Atlas · Atom Software · Strategic Overview · 2025                                                               12
It works like a set of tripwires. If a song suddenly gets 10 times its normal streams in a single day,
that's a hard flag — the suspicious streams are removed and the situation is reviewed before the
chart runs. If streams are all coming from the same internet address block, or from brand-new
accounts, those are also flagged. Soft cases (like an unusually low skip rate) reduce the stream
count rather than remove it entirely.

Every removal is logged with evidence — the specific numbers that triggered the rule. This log is
what a third-party auditor checks when they verify the chart. Without this, IFPI won't grant official
recognition.



Why the Charts Unlock So Much Else
The chart itself has obvious value — it's the cultural record of what Mongolia is listening to. But
the indirect value is much larger:

 • Atlas Sound: Artists signed to Atlas Sound can now point to an official, audited chart position —
    not just a streaming number. This is credible to international labels, sync licensors, and booking
    agents.

 • Atlas Live: Chart position determines billing at events. The #1 song on the Mongolia Hot 100 is
    the headline act. The chart creates a legitimate ranking system for talent booking.

 • Atlas Data: Labels, brands, and PR agencies need chart position data to measure campaign
    success. Atom sells this as a B2B subscription.

 • Media legitimacy: Every Mongolian news outlet covering music needs a source for chart
    positions. Atlas becomes that source — driving significant traffic and brand authority for Atlas Post
    and Atlas Media.

 • International recognition: A Billboard-recognised chart opens the door to Mongolian artists
    appearing on Billboard's global charts. That is a completely different level of international visibility
    for Mongolian music.

The Box Office —
BoxOffice.mn
Mongolia's first official box office — why this is a faster win than the music charts and
how it opens doors with film distributors and studios.




The Opportunity
No one has ever officially tracked Mongolian cinema ticket sales. There is no
box office. The gross of every film that runs in Mongolia is simply unknown — or
known only to the cinema and the distributor in a private, unverifiable way.


   W H Y T H I S I S T H E FA S T E S T W I N I N T H E P O RT F O L I O

   BoxOffice.mn doesn't require IFPI, doesn't require DSP partnerships, and doesn't require an
   association. You need to convince two or three Mongolian cinema chains to connect their point-
   of-sale systems to Atom's platform — and in return, they get free marketing (their films appear
   in the official rankings) and a professional analytics dashboard.


   The cinema integration is technically simple: most POS systems can export a nightly sales file.
   Anand builds an ingest system; your full-stack friend builds the public-facing site and cinema
   dashboard. You handle the cinema conversations. This can go live within the first phase of
   building.




What BoxOffice.mn Provides
 • Real-time ranking of the top films in Mongolia by ticket revenue — the first definitive public record
    of its kind

 • Opening weekend gross, cumulative gross, per-screen averages for each film

 • Historical database — once cinemas adopt the system, you can build the archive backward from
    there

 • B2B dashboard for film distributors (UIP, Warner, local distributors) — the analytics they've never
    had, free in exchange for participation

 • Embeddable widgets for Mongolian entertainment media — Atlas Post publishes the weekend chart,
    other outlets embed it

The Bigger Play
Once BoxOffice.mn is the recognised source for Mongolian cinema data, Atlas Data can sell
subscriptions to the analytics feed to international studios, distributors, and market research
firms. Mongolia is a growing cinema market that international players have almost no visibility
into. Atlas can be the window.

There is also a path to UNIC (Union Internationale des Cinémas) and NATO affiliate recognition —
the same international framework that IFPI provides for music. Atlas Charts and BoxOffice.mn
become parallel institutions: one for music, one for film, both operated by Atom, both
internationally recognised.

Key Decisions Only You Can Make
These are not engineering questions. They are strategic choices that determine what kind of
institution Atlas becomes. Decide them before building starts.


   D E C I S I O N 1 — C H A RT M E T H O D O LO GY O W N E R S H I P

   The chart formula (which sources are weighted how much) is a public document that defines
   Atlas's credibility. You need to write this, own it, and stand behind it. Anand implements
   whatever the methodology document says — but you decide what the methodology says. This is
   the editorial equivalent of a newspaper's editorial standards — it's your responsibility, not the
   engineers'.

   DECISION 2 — ISRC REGISTRANT FEE STRUCTURE

   You need to decide how much it costs for a label to register as an ISRC registrant through MPIA.
   This has to be cheap enough to not create a barrier (otherwise artists will keep using
   international distributors and you won't build the registry) but not so cheap that it signals no
   institutional value. Recommended starting point: free for founding year, then a modest annual
   fee (₮50,000–₮150,000 range) from year 2.




   D E C I S I O N 3 — C H A RT L AU N C H D AT E

   There is tension between launching early (to establish the brand before any competitor moves)
   and launching complete (to launch with full data coverage and credibility). The right answer is:
   launch with YouTube + ACRCloud only, clearly labelled as "Streaming & Airplay Chart," with a
   published roadmap for adding Spotify and Apple Music. Don't wait for full coverage. The first
   mover advantage is real.




   D E C I S I O N 4 — T S A RT S A A N A M J I L ' S R O L E I N T H E C H A RT

   The chart announcement is a content format that needs to be defined. Is it a video show? A
   newsletter? A social post? A combination? Who decides the chart comment — does Tsartsaa
   Namjil editorially comment on the chart, or is it purely data? This is a creative and editorial
   decision, not a technical one, and it shapes the entire public face of Atlas Charts.
